Standard Operating Procedure

Two principles underpin everything below

  • Coaches observe; the manager speaks. Coaches do not deliver approval, denial, or refund decisions to the player on court. They send the call to the manager, who logs it and contacts the player. One voice avoids on-court promises that contradict the shared record.
  • The app never blocks. Entries always save. Conflicting decisions are reconciled by talking to the coaches first — not by overwriting the record afterward.
Player on court Which scenario are you in? Everything that comes through this app fits one of the two branches below.
Scenario A

Player seeks 4.0+ approval

Triggered by a player request — usually a pre-class email, or an existing clinic player asking to be approved for a higher level.

  1. Receive the request and flag the coach in advance

    Requests typically arrive one of two ways:

    • Pre-class email from the player asking to be approved for 4.0–4.5 or 4.5.
      • Before the class, post in the coaches' chat: player name, level being evaluated, date and time — so the assigned coach knows who to watch for.
    • Existing clinic player asking a coach or the front desk to be moved up.
  2. Observe the flagged player through the class Coach
  3. Form the call silently — do not tell the player Coach

    Reach a conclusion in your head: approval or denial. Do not deliver the verdict on court.

    • If asked how they did: "I'll send my notes to the manager and they'll be in touch."
    • Don't promise an approval or hint at one.
    • Don't deliver denial feedback face-to-face — the manager sends it in the follow-up email so wording is consistent and on record.
  4. Report the decision to the manager — same day Coach

    Coaches' chat (preferred), in person, or email. Include:

    • Player name (roster spelling).
    • Outcome — Approved or Denied.
    • Level(s) — including the 4.5 verdict if the call is "Approved for 4.0–4.5".
    • Feedback (required for denials) — concrete: strokes, movement, match play.
  5. Look up the player first Manager

    Open the Look up player tab. Check approval status per gated level (Approved / Denied / Disagreement) and the full history.

  6. Reconcile conflicts before logging Manager

    If the new call contradicts what's on record, raise it in the coaches' chat and get alignment first. The app will save a contradiction without complaint — surface it before logging.

  7. Log the entry (Log entry tab) Manager
    • Pick the coach who made the call, the player, and the entry type.
    • Approving for 4.0–4.5 also requires a 4.5 verdict. The app enforces this.
    • Paste the coach's feedback for denials.
  8. Email the player Manager
    Outcome?
    Approved Email the player. "You can join 4.5 now" or "stay in 4.0–4.5 for now" based on the 4.5 verdict, with registration instructions.
    Denied Email the player with the coach's feedback — concrete enough that they know what to work on and what's next. Don't leave them without word; the coach was silent on court by design.
Scenario B

Player showed up at the wrong level

Mismatch noticed during warm-up or early drills.

  1. Act on court — pick the path that fits Coach

    You have roughly the first 15–20 minutes to decide. Past that, the disruption is locked in.

    Gap < 1.0 Finish the class as planned. Report to the manager later — no on-court action needed.
    Gap ≥ 1.0 · Suitable class available Check the court sheet (same time or within the next few hours), kindly redirect the player to that court, and notify the front desk to update attendance.
    Gap ≥ 1.0 · No suitable class Use the neutral script below. Do not promise a refund or quote eligibility — refunds are the manager's call. "I want to make sure you have the best experience. This group isn't the right level today. Our manager will be in touch by email — please email manager@rippnertennis.com if you'd like to follow up directly."
  2. Report to the manager — same day Coach
    • Player name (roster spelling).
    • Level they attended and level they should be in.
    • Feedback — which class they showed up to, what tipped you off.
    • Whether you were able to redirect them on court or not.
  3. Look up the player — first or repeat? Manager

    Open the Look up player tab. The wrong-class history drives everything that follows — confirm first vs. repeat occurrence before acting.

  4. Log the entry Manager
    • Pick the coach who made the call, the player, and "Showed up at wrong class".
    • Pick level attended and level they should be in.
    • Paste the coach's feedback (required).
  5. Player follow-up Manager
    First or repeat occurrence?
    First occurrence
    • Add a note to the player's profile (external system of record) documenting the level concern.
    • Follow-up email to the player within 24 hours.
    • Review refund eligibility — a credit is issued only if the player has never been flagged before and has never received an exception.
    Second occurrence
    • Add a profile note, clearly flagging the repeat occurrence.
    • Escalate to senior management for approval before:
      • Removing the "Guest Group" permission, or
      • Sending any formal Code of Conduct email.
    • Follow-up email to the player within 24 hours.

Where notes live

The app is the alert and shared decision history — it is append-only and has no in-app editing. Ongoing player notes (profile notes, refund decisions, permission changes) are maintained in the external system of record. The app tells you what happened and whether it has happened before; the external system holds the resolution.
